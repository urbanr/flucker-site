1. Two panels, one keyboard

Two panels side by side; ⇥ Tab switches between them. The function bar at the bottom shows what F2–F8 do – and what they do with ⌥, ⇧ or ⌘ while you hold it. ↩ opens, ⌫ goes up, Space marks, ⌘E shows the same folder in the other panel, ⇧⌘G goes to any folder by typing its path. Start typing a name to jump to it.

2. Welcome to Flucker

Shown at the first start and always in Window › Welcome to Flucker…: the full-text engine (ⓘ compares both with measurements), appearance, what the places menu shows, your first three favourites, disk access, local telemetry and the main keys.

3. Command palette

⇧⌘P finds any command by a few letters of its name, shows its key and runs it. It also finds files of the index and your snippets, so it is the fastest way to anything you do not have a key for.

4. Keyboard shortcuts overview

⌘' lists every command with its keys, grouped as in the menus, and searches them. Commands without a key are listed too, so you know they exist. The same overview is in Settings › Keys.

5. Tabs and workspaces

Each panel has its own tabs (⌘T); a tab can be locked so it never leaves its folder. A workspace remembers the tabs of both panels under a name – switch your whole setup for a project in one step.

6. Places and favourites

⌥F1 and ⌥F2 (or the icon at the start of a panel's path) open the places of the left or right panel: home, volumes, network, Trash, favourites and workspaces, each with its letter. ⇧⌘D adds the current folder to favourites. In the App Store edition Flucker opens only folders you have granted; Window › Granted Access lists them.

7. Archives and documents as folders

↩ steps into archives (ZIP, TAR, RAR, CAB, ISO and more) as if they were folders; copy files out of them with F5 as usual. Many other files open the same way with ⌥↩, while ↩ still opens them in their app: Office and OpenDocument documents, EPUB, Pages/Numbers/Keynote, app packages (JAR, APK, IPA, DEB, RPM…), SQLite databases, JSON and property lists, PDF, mailboxes, Photoshop layers, Matroska video, network captures and disk images. The context menu's Enter Inside shows which key does it for the file under the cursor. ⌥F5 packs, ⌥F9 unpacks.

8. Copy, move and the Dock

F5 copies and F6 moves the marked items (or the one under the cursor) to the other panel; F7 makes a folder, F8 moves to the Trash, ⇧F8 deletes. Operations run in the Dock below the panels: pause, cancel, and decide conflicts there – one by one or for all at once.

9. History and undo

Window › Operation History lists what was copied, moved, renamed or deleted; an operation can be undone from there. Flucker records every step in a journal, so an operation interrupted by a crash or power loss is finished or reported truthfully at the next start.

10. Find files

⌥F7 searches by name, size, dates, kind and – with the index – by content, with snippets of the text found. Results go to a panel (⌘↩), can be previewed with F3, and a search can be saved as a smart folder. ⌥⌘F filters the panel by name as you type.

11. Full-text index

Window › Index chooses the places whose content Flucker indexes and shows how far it got. Two engines: SQLite FTS5 (default) and Tantivy, 1.4–9.3× faster when searching the whole index – Settings › Index, ⓘ shows the measurement. The index stays on your Mac.

12. Smart folders

A smart folder is a saved query – conditions on name, kind, size, dates, tags or content – shown as a folder in a panel and kept up to date. Make one with File › New Smart Folder… or from a search.

13. Colours and icons

Rules in Settings › Panels give files and folders their colour and icon by name, extension, kind or tag – sources in one colour, archives in another, your project folders with their own icon. The first rule that matches wins.

14. Appearance

Settings › Appearance: follow macOS or keep one profile; edit any colour of the built-in dark and light profiles, the transparency of the windows and the fonts; export and import profiles.

15. Preview

F3 shows the file under the cursor: text and code, Markdown as formatted text, tables, images and whatever Quick Look can show. F4 opens the file in your editor.

16. Storage usage

Window › Storage Usage draws a folder or a whole volume as rings – the bigger the slice, the more space it takes. Click to go deeper; the scan runs in the background and is remembered for next time.

17. Clipboard history and snippets

⇧⌘V shows what you copied in Flucker and pastes any of it again. Snippets (⇧⌘S) keep texts you type often – paths, commands, replies – and paste them by a short abbreviation, also from the command palette.

18. Compare, sync and batch rename

⇧F2 compares the folders of both panels, shows what differs and copies it one way or both ways. File › Batch Rename… renames many files at once by a pattern, with a preview of every new name before anything changes.

19. Telemetry and diagnostics

Telemetry measures the app's response times and resources – off by default, kept on your Mac for 30 days, never sent. Help › Diagnostics (hold ⌥) shows the edition, journals and telemetry; Export Diagnostics… saves a file without any names of files or paths, shown to you whole before it is saved.

20. Languages

Flucker speaks 36 languages – most European languages, Japanese, Chinese (Simplified) and Hindi. Settings › General › Language follows macOS or keeps the one you choose; Flucker restarts in it and keeps your panels.
