1. Two panels, one keyboard

Flucker shows two panels side by side. ⇥ Tab switches between them. The function bar at the bottom shows what the keys F2 to F8 do. Hold ⌥, ⇧ or ⌘ and it shows what they do with that key. ↩ opens, ⌫ goes up and Space marks. ⌘E shows the same folder in the other panel. ⇧⌘G goes to any folder by its path. Start typing a name to jump to it.

2. Welcome to Flucker

This window opens at the first start. You find it later in Window › Welcome to Flucker…. It sets the full-text engine, the appearance, what the places menu shows, your first three favourites, disk access and local telemetry. It also lists the main keys. The ⓘ button compares both full-text engines with real measurements.

3. Command palette

⇧⌘P finds any command by a few letters of its name. It shows the key of the command and runs it. The palette also finds files of the index and your snippets. It is the fastest way to anything that has no key.

4. Keyboard shortcuts overview

⌘' lists every command with its keys. The commands are grouped as in the menus and you can search them. Commands without a key are listed too, so you know they exist. The same overview is in Settings › Keys.

5. Tabs and workspaces

Each panel has its own tabs. ⌘T opens a new one. A locked tab never leaves its folder. A workspace remembers the tabs of both panels under a name. Switch to it and your whole setup for a project is back in one step.

6. Places and favourites

⌥F1 opens the places of the left panel and ⌥F2 those of the right one. The icon at the start of a panel's path does the same. The places are your home, volumes, network, Trash, favourites and workspaces, each with its own letter. ⇧⌘D adds the current folder to favourites. The App Store edition opens only the folders you have granted. Window › Granted Access lists them.

7. Archives and documents as folders

↩ steps into archives such as ZIP, TAR, RAR, CAB and ISO as if they were folders. Copy files out of them with F5 as usual. Many other files open the same way with ⌥↩, while ↩ still opens them in their app. These are Office and OpenDocument documents, EPUB, Pages, Numbers and Keynote, app packages such as JAR, APK, IPA, DEB and RPM, SQLite databases, JSON and property lists, PDF, mailboxes, Photoshop layers, Matroska video, network captures and disk images. The context menu item Enter Inside shows which key does it for the file under the cursor. ⌥F5 packs and ⌥F9 unpacks.

8. Copy, move and the Dock

F5 copies and F6 moves the marked items to the other panel. Without marks they act on the item under the cursor. F7 makes a folder. F8 moves to the Trash and ⇧F8 deletes. Operations run in the Dock below the panels. Pause or cancel them there and decide conflicts one by one or for all at once.

9. History and undo

Window › Operation History lists what was copied, moved, renamed or deleted. An operation can be undone from there. Flucker records every step in a journal. If a crash or a power loss interrupts an operation, the next start finishes it or tells you truthfully what was not done.

10. Find files

⌥F7 searches by name, size, dates and kind. With the index it also searches the content and shows snippets of the text found. ⌘↩ sends the results to a panel and F3 previews them. A search can be saved as a smart folder. ⌥⌘F filters the panel by name as you type.

11. Full-text index

Window › Index chooses the places whose content Flucker indexes and shows how far it got. There are two engines. SQLite FTS5 is the default. Tantivy is 1.4 to 9.3 times faster when searching the whole index. Choose the engine in Settings › Index, where ⓘ shows the measurement. The index stays on your Mac.

12. Smart folders

A smart folder is a saved query. Its conditions use the name, kind, size, dates, tags or content of files. A panel shows it as a folder and keeps it up to date. Make one with File › New Smart Folder… or from a search.

13. Colours and icons

Rules in Settings › Panels give files and folders their colour and icon. A rule matches the name, extension, kind or tag. Sources can have one colour, archives another and your project folders their own icon. The first rule that matches wins.

14. Appearance

In Settings › Appearance Flucker follows macOS or keeps one profile. You can edit any colour of the built-in dark and light profiles, the transparency of the windows and the fonts. Profiles can be exported and imported.

15. Preview

F3 shows the file under the cursor. It shows text and code, Markdown as formatted text, tables, images and whatever Quick Look can show. F4 opens the file in your editor.

16. Storage usage

Window › Storage Usage draws a folder or a whole volume as rings. The bigger the slice, the more space it takes. Click a slice to go deeper. The scan runs in the background and is remembered for next time.

17. Clipboard history and snippets

⇧⌘V shows what you copied in Flucker and pastes any of it again. Snippets (⇧⌘S) keep texts you type often, such as paths, commands or replies. Paste a snippet by its short abbreviation, also from the command palette.

18. Compare, sync and batch rename

⇧F2 compares the folders of both panels and shows what differs. Flucker then copies the differences one way or both ways. File › Batch Rename… renames many files at once by a pattern. It previews every new name before anything changes.

19. Telemetry and diagnostics

Telemetry measures the response times and resources of the app. It is off by default. When it is on, the measurements stay on your Mac for 30 days and are never sent. Hold ⌥ and choose Help › Diagnostics to see the edition, the journals and the telemetry. Export Diagnostics… saves a file without any names of files or paths. You see the whole file before it is saved.

20. Languages

Flucker speaks 36 languages. These are most European languages, Japanese, Chinese (Simplified) and Hindi. Settings › General › Language follows macOS or keeps the one you choose. Flucker then restarts in that language and keeps your panels.
